How to write a weekly report your team will actually read

MS

Maren Solberg

Product lead · November 3, 2026 · 7 min read

On this page

  1. Why most reports go unread
  2. Lead with what changed
  3. Name the risks early
  4. A template you can copy
  5. Make it a habit

Most weekly reports are written for the person writing them. They list everything that happened, in the order it happened, and nobody gets past the third bullet. Here is what we learned from reading about 3,000 of them while building Fjordly’s report.

Why most reports go unread

A report gets read when the reader can tell in ten seconds whether they need to act. Long lists of finished tasks hide the two lines that matter: what is late and what someone needs to decide.

Lead with what changed

Start with one sentence that compares this week to the plan. Then keep each section to three items at most:

  • Done: outcomes, not activity. “Onboarding live at all 4 hubs”, not “worked on onboarding”.
  • Moved: anything whose date changed, with the new date.
  • Next: what the team commits to by next Friday.

Name the risks early

A risk named on Friday is a plan. The same risk found on Wednesday is a surprise. Give each one an owner and the date you will know more.

“If a report never says ‘at risk’, it isn’t telling you the truth yet.”

A template you can copy

Paste this into a doc, or into a Fjordly report template, and fill it in on Friday afternoon:

## Week of Nov 2: on track, 1 risk

Done
- Peak season staffing plan signed off
- Driver onboarding live at all 4 hubs

Moved
- Route planner v2: Nov 6 → Nov 10 (waiting on map data)

At risk
- Q1 carrier contracts. Owner: Maren. Know more by: Nov 12

Next
- Ship route planner v2 to Oslo and Bergen

Make it a habit

Send it at the same time every week, to the same channel, even when the week was quiet. After a month, people start reading it before the Monday meeting, and the meeting gets shorter on its own.
